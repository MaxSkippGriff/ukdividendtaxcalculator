Are Dividends Inside an ISA Taxed?

No — dividends received inside a Stocks and Shares ISA are completely free from UK income tax and dividend tax. They don't count towards the £500 dividend allowance either. This guide explains how ISA dividends differ from taxable dividends held outside an ISA.

Published by the UK Money Calculators editorial team. Last updated for the 2026/27 tax year.

How ISA dividend protection works

A Stocks and Shares ISA acts as a tax-free wrapper around your investments. Any dividends paid on shares or funds held inside the ISA remain within that wrapper and are never subject to income tax or dividend tax — regardless of how large those dividends are or what tax band you are in.

ISA dividends also do not appear on your Self Assessment return and do not affect your dividend allowance. They are, in effect, invisible to the UK dividend tax system.

ISA allowance for 2026/27

You can pay up to £20,000 into ISAs in total across the 2026/27 tax year. This can be split across a Cash ISA, Stocks and Shares ISA, Innovative Finance ISA and Lifetime ISA (the Lifetime ISA has a separate £4,000 limit within the overall £20,000).

Crucially, once money is inside the ISA, any dividends earned on that money are not capped or limited. A large portfolio accumulated over many years can generate significant dividend income entirely free of tax.

Worked example — ISA vs outside ISA

Scenario: An investor with a salary of £30,000 receives £5,000 in dividends from a share portfolio in 2026/27.

Inside a Stocks and Shares ISA:

  • £5,000 dividends — completely tax-free inside the ISA.
  • Dividend tax: £0.

Outside an ISA (in a general investment account):

  • Salary of £30,000 uses the basic-rate band. Dividends sit on top.
  • First £500: dividend allowance — £0 tax.
  • Remaining £4,500 at 8.75% = £484.
  • Dividend tax: approximately £484.

The same £5,000 in dividends costs nothing inside an ISA but costs £484 outside one.

Common confusion

  • ISA dividends count towards the £500 allowance — they don't. The dividend allowance applies only to dividends outside the ISA wrapper. ISA dividends are entirely separate and do not reduce your allowance.
  • You can transfer dividends from outside an ISA into one. You cannot do this directly — cash dividends received outside an ISA would count as a new subscription if paid into an ISA. To benefit from ISA protection, the investment itself must be held inside the ISA.
  • ISA dividends need to be reported on Self Assessment. They don't. ISA dividends are tax-free and not reportable. You only declare dividends received outside the ISA wrapper.

Frequently asked questions

Are dividends inside an ISA taxed?
No. Dividends received inside a Stocks and Shares ISA are completely free from UK income tax and dividend tax. There is no limit on the amount of dividends you can earn tax-free within your ISA, provided your annual ISA subscription is within the £20,000 limit.
Do ISA dividends count towards the £500 dividend allowance?
No. ISA dividends are entirely outside the dividend tax system. They do not count towards the £500 dividend allowance and do not affect how much dividend tax you owe on dividends outside the ISA.
Can I put my dividends into an ISA?
You cannot move dividends already received outside an ISA into one after the fact — that would count as a new ISA subscription. However, dividends paid on investments held inside a Stocks and Shares ISA automatically stay within the ISA wrapper and are tax-free.
Do ISA dividends affect my Self Assessment?
No. Dividends received inside an ISA do not need to be reported on a Self Assessment return. They are tax-free and outside the dividend tax system entirely.
Is there a limit on how much dividend I can earn inside an ISA?
There is no limit on the dividends you can earn tax-free inside an ISA. The only limit is on annual subscriptions (£20,000 for 2026/27). Growth and income inside the ISA, including dividends, are not subject to any additional cap.
